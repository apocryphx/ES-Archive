Privacy

ES Archive Server collects nothing. Here is exactly where everything goes.

Where your entries are stored

In a database inside ES Archive Server's own sandbox container on this Mac. The app is sandboxed, so it reaches its own container and the files you explicitly hand it through a Save or Open panel — and nothing else on your disk.

What leaves this Mac

In normal use, one thing: iCloud sync, if you are signed in. The archive syncs through your own private CloudKit database (iCloud.com.elarity.electric-sheep) under your Apple Account, which is what makes the same entries available on your other Macs. It goes to Apple, under your account, and nowhere else. If you are not signed into iCloud, nothing leaves at all and the app works exactly the same.

There is no other network activity. No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no update check, no third-party service.

How your AI reaches the archive

ES Archive Server listens on 127.0.0.1 — the loopback interface — and nowhere else. Only processes on this Mac can connect, one listener per persona. Nothing on your local network, and nothing on the internet, can reach it.

The one exception is one you create deliberately: if you put a persona behind a cloudflared tunnel of your own, that traffic travels under your Cloudflare account and its authentication. ES Archive Server opens no remote access on its own, and you can require a Cloudflare Access token per port — see Personas.

Semantic search runs here

The model that turns entries into searchable vectors is bundled inside the app and runs on this Mac's own hardware. No text is ever sent anywhere to be embedded, searched, or indexed — not even to Apple.

What is collected

Nothing. Specifically:

  • No analytics or telemetry are sent anywhere.
  • No data is shared with the developer or with any third party.
  • No third-party servers are contacted.
  • Diagnostic messages are written only to this Mac's own system log.

Keeping your own copy

File ▸ Back Up… writes the whole archive to a single .esarchive file wherever you choose. It is an ordinary file — store it, encrypt it, or delete it as you see fit. See Backing up and restoring.

Removing everything

Deleting the app removes its container and the archive with it. If iCloud sync is on, the copy in your private CloudKit database is removed through iCloud settings for your Apple Account, the same as any other app's iCloud data. Back up first if you want to keep anything.

The full privacy policy is at github.com/apocryphx/ES-Archive.
