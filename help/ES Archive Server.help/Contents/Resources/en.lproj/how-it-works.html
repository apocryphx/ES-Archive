How ES Archive Server works

A memory your AI writes, keeps, and searches — on your Mac.

Most AI assistants forget everything when a conversation ends. ES Archive Server gives one an archive that outlives the session: it can write something down, find it again weeks later, connect it to related notes, and prune what has gone stale.

The important thing to understand is who does the writing. You do not type entries into ES Archive Server. Your AI does — through the tools this app exposes, while you are having an ordinary conversation with it. ES Archive Server is the archive and the instrument panel; the assistant is the author.

The three parts

PartWhat it is
The archiveA database on this Mac holding every entry, tag, link and revision.
The engineThe part your AI talks to. It stores, searches, links and forgets on the assistant's instruction.
The appWhat you see: Archive Scope, Settings, backup and restore, and the window that connects a client.

What an entry is

Each entry carries a title, a short summary, a body, a type, and the name of the persona that wrote it. On top of that it can accumulate tags, links to other entries, comments added later, references to outside sources, and a trail of revisions recording how it changed.

The summary matters more than it looks. It is what semantic search actually matches against, so an entry stored without one is invisible to search and shows up as an isolated node in Archive Scope.

How search finds things

Every entry is converted into a vector — a numeric fingerprint of its meaning — by a model that runs entirely on this Mac (EmbeddingGemma, 768 dimensions, quantised to int4, executed through Core ML). Searching compares fingerprints rather than words, so a query finds an entry that means the same thing in different words.

The model is multilingual across more than a hundred languages. A question asked in German can reach an entry written in English, and the reverse.

Nothing is sent away to be embedded

Text never leaves this Mac for the sake of search. The model is bundled inside the app and runs locally, offline, every time.

Where entries live

The archive is a Core Data (SQLite) store inside the app's own sandbox container on this Mac. If you are signed into iCloud it also syncs through your private CloudKit database, so the same archive is available on your other Macs. If you are not signed into iCloud, everything simply stays here and the app works exactly the same.

Nothing is sent to the developer or to any third party. See Privacy for the full picture, and Backing up and restoring for how to keep your own copy.

What you actually do

  1. Connect the app you chat with — once.
  2. Talk to your assistant normally. It stores and recalls as it goes.
  3. Open Archive Scope when you want to see what the archive has become.
  4. Back up now and then.
