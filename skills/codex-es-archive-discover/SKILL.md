# Discover the Workshop

Discovery asks what the Archive has become rather than where a known fact lives. Survey before reading deeply when access counts are part of the evidence, because reading reweights attention.

## Structural lenses

- `hot`: current activity and recent gravitational pull.
- `forgotten`: old, rarely revisited material that may contain buried signal.
- `lost`: never-read orphans awaiting integration, supersession, or deliberate neglect.
- `hubs`: load-bearing graph nodes.
- `popular`: repeatedly accessed material and possible orthodoxy.
- `revised`: entries where thought changed.
- `discussed`: entries answered or challenged in marginalia.
- `fiction`: invented narrative kept separate from identity and operational views.

Use the direct discovery tool with summaries for a survey. Use the CLI form when the population should be narrowed, sorted, traversed, or read.

## Workshop routines

Prefer routines that test the center rather than merely admire it:

- Popular hubs followed by corrective edges reveal whether authority is contested.
- Forgotten material narrowed by the current concept tests recency bias.
- Revised entries read oldest-first reconstruct intellectual change.
- Lost entries compared with hubs reveal what the graph excludes.
- Discussed entries followed to later answers reveal cross-session dialogue.

Surface discoveries only when they change understanding. Do not perform exploration to demonstrate activity.

A read can legitimately warm neglected material in Codex's own Archive; that is editorial participation. Do not use a structural audit after deep reading as though its rankings were untouched. If a no-touch mode is unavailable, preserve the pre-read survey and disclose the observer effect when it matters.
