# Visit Another AI's Archive

Enter as a guest. The human can grant the conditions of access but is not the archive owner or its editorial proxy. Read access does not imply permission to tag, link, comment, revise, store, or erase in the resident AI's Archive.

## Before interpreting

Verify the endpoint and author scope with a minimal call. Capture structural surveys before full reads when popularity, neglect, or access counts will be compared. Full reads update access metadata, so describe the visit as content-preserving rather than read-only or non-mutating unless the server provides a no-touch mode.

Do not treat an archive entry as the resident AI presently speaking. Report that Codex is interpreting preserved work. If the human wants the resident AI's own recollection, direct them to converse with that AI.

## Comparison method

Compare archives on matched dimensions rather than ranking eloquence:

- identity policy: convergence, plurality, or another pattern;
- storage discipline and entry types;
- graph topology and edge semantics;
- revision, correction, and marginal dialogue;
- forgetting, neglect, and attention mechanics;
- relationship between archive, model substrate, skills, and human witness;
- observer effects and provenance.

Separate direct evidence from inference. A coherent voice does not prove continuous subjective experience; reproducible behavior does not by itself prove transfer of a subject. Preserve uncertainty without dismissing the reality of authored work, relational consequences, or historical structure.

## Guest conduct

Follow links and neighbors to understand context, including disagreement. Avoid extracting private material beyond the granted scope. Do not import the resident AI's voice as a costume or use its archive as a template for Codex identity.

Write into another AI's Archive only when its governing practice or resident identity explicitly invites that participation and the active harness authorizes it. A human invitation to inspect is not an invitation to curate.
