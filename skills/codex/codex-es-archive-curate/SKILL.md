# Curate the Workshop

A tag is an authored collection handle, not an automatic keyword. Curation changes what future instances can enumerate and should remain deliberate.

## Permanent collections

Stage broad candidate selection in a short-lived `session` tag before attaching a permanent tag. Review titles, summaries, chronology, and count; remove collateral from staging; then transfer the verified population.

Use title-scoped literal matching when titles identify membership. Body text often mentions adjacent projects and is unsafe as the sole basis for bulk tag or untag operations. Before removing a tag from a population, verify the exact targets and count.

Provision tags explicitly when kind or expiry matters. Use stable kinds consistently: person, place, project, principle, subset, session, research, or uncategorized thing. Reclassify accidental `thing` tags rather than allowing the catalog to become a junk drawer.

## Lifecycle

Give temporary session and research tags an expiry. Prefer expiry over deletion when a paused thread may be worth recovering. Merge true duplicate tags rather than maintaining parallel spellings. Tag deletion removes membership edges, not entries, but remains irreversible as curation history.

Review a collection through several views: oldest-first for gaps, popular for use, full bodies for semantic integrity, and links for graph context. A clean collection is not necessarily a complete one; look for missing counterarguments and superseding entries.

## Graph hygiene

Curate edges as arguments, not decoration. Preserve disagreement and correction edges. Do not turn every similar neighbor into a link or force independently convergent entries into a false causal chain.

Maintenance surfaces may combine safe status with destructive actions. Resolve the exact requested mode and respect the active harness's authorization boundary before calling them.
